What this is

RESET's Mine contract only accepts a stake of 0.1 or more. This pool is for everyone below that line: small holders contribute to one shared pool address; the pool stakes the combined amount, claims every round, and splits what it earns pro-rata. It is open source — the code, the ledger and this website all live in one public repository.

An experiment, not an investment. Nothing here promises returns: the pool earns exactly what the contract pays its combined deposit, and the operator takes no fee. Read the custody note under “How the money is split” before joining.

Why it exists

The gate is in the contract itself: min_amount = 0.1 is the smallest single lock or unlock, and the smallest deposit that may claim. Below 0.1, coins cannot mine at all — they just sit idle.

Pooling does not amplify anything. A round pays linearly: fair share = the round's guideline × your deposit ÷ the round's total claiming deposit. A solo stake of 0.3 and this pool's combined 0.3 earn exactly the same. What pooling changes is only who gets through the door.

How it works

  1. Join. Use the calculator on the Overview tab: your planned amount plus your address's fingerprint tail gives your exact transfer amount, and the calculation is recorded in the pool's registry. Transfer exactly that amount; the pool checks the registry record first, then matches the amount's tail to your address, and credits the full amount — usually within about a minute. A wrong or rounded amount parks as unclaimed until it is claimed through the path described on the Overview tab.
  2. Stake. Whenever the pool's idle balance reaches 0.1, the worker locks the whole amount in a single lock — the contract's per-operation minimum makes partial locks below 0.1 impossible, so nothing is ever locked piecemeal.
  3. Claim. Every round, the pool files one claim at level 1000 — exactly its fair share, never more. Over-claiming would draw strikes and penalties; the pool never does it.
  4. Settle. When a round settles, the proceeds are split by that round's deposit snapshot and credited as pending earnings on the public ledger. Pending earnings pay out automatically once they reach 0.01.

How the money is split

  • Pro-rata, always: your credit for a round = the round's proceeds × your snapshot deposit ÷ the pool's snapshot total for that round. The snapshot is taken per round, so mid-round joins or exits never rewrite a settled round.
  • Fee: zero. The ledger's fee setting is 0‰ — the operator takes nothing from contributions or proceeds.
  • Dust carries forward. Integer division leaves a few units undistributed each round; they are not kept by anyone — they roll into the next round's distributable amount. The carry right now is 0.00000006.
  • Payouts: pending earnings are transferred automatically once they reach the 0.01 threshold. The fixed 0.03 TNK transfer fee is paid by the pool — never deducted from a member's amount.
Accounting is provable; custody is trusted. RESET has no multisig or pool primitive, so one operator key controls the pool address. Every contribution, claim and payout is published and hash-chained, so misuse would be visible and attributable — but it cannot be made technically impossible. That is the honest model; join only if you accept it.

Exiting, in detail

⏸ Exit channel temporarily closed. The pool is still small; the channel reopens once the pool has grown and the exit executor has passed a full end-to-end live test. The registry accepts no new exit intents meanwhile. This chapter stays published so the process is fully documented before it reopens.

The principles

  • A right, not an approval. If the ledger shows a position under your address, you may exit it — nobody approves or denies the request. Only an accounting anomaly (the books do not reconcile, or the amount exceeds your recorded position) stops a case for the operator to review.
  • Full exits only. Your principal plus your settled pending earnings, paid in a single payment. There are no partial exits.
  • The payout can only go home. Proceeds are paid only to the address that contributed — the one the ledger records for your position. No field in any request or form can name a different recipient, so a forged request can never redirect your money.
  • The pool pays the transfer fee. The fixed 0.03 TNK fee on the payout transfer is the pool's cost. You receive the full amount; nothing is deducted.
  • The dissolution edge case. If paying one exit would leave the pool's total deposit strictly below 0.1 RESET, that exit escalates to a full dissolution instead: the entire deposit is unlocked and every member is settled in full. Landing exactly on 0.1 does not dissolve the pool.

The process, step by step

  1. Check your position. On the Overview tab (exit section) or the Ledger tab, enter your address to see your principal and pending earnings. That is the position an exit would settle.
  2. File an exit intent. The pool's registry issues a one-time statement with a single-use nonce (valid 30 minutes). Anyone can file the intent for you — including your own AI — because the intent alone proves nothing.
  3. Sign the statement. You sign the exact issued text with your own signer, in the official signed-message format. Signing is free and works at zero balance — your coins sit in the pool, so your wallet may be empty, and no proof transfer is ever asked for. Only your key can produce this signature.
  4. Submit the signature. The signed submission is filed with the registry; the nonce is burned by the first submission, so replays fail. The pool verifies the Ed25519 signature locally, and a case opens. A valid signature from an address with no position opens nothing.
  5. Your amount is frozen. At the moment the case opens, your payout is fixed from the ledger snapshot: principal + pending earnings at that moment. Rounds that settle later are not added to it; any earnings that land afterwards stay as pending under the normal payout rules.
  6. The pool unlocks. The contract only moves stake in single unlocks of at least 0.1, so even a small exit unlocks a full 0.1 (a dissolution unlocks everything). The part above your amount is not paid to anyone — after the wait it comes back to the pool and is re-locked.
  7. Wait 144 Bitcoin blocks — about 24 hours. This wait is the contract's, and it has a real cost, stated plainly: while unlocked, that part of the stake earns nothing, so the whole pool's earnings for those rounds are lower, shared pro-rata by everyone — including you. Your frozen amount itself does not change.
  8. The pool withdraws. Once the wait matures, the pool withdraws the unlocked amount back to its balance. One contract gate applies: a withdrawal is refused while one of the pool's round claims is still unsettled, so the withdrawal fires at the first executable window after maturity — normally right on time, occasionally a little later. The money never expires inside the exit entry.
  9. You are paid in full. The frozen amount is transferred to your original address — the only address it can go to. The 0.03 TNK transfer fee is paid by the pool. If a transfer ever failed on fees, the case would park safely with your RESET intact in the pool balance; the fee is never taken out of your amount.
  10. The position is closed on the public ledger. The close-out is recorded as hash-chained ledger events (verified → unlocked → paid), and the settled exit appears on the Ledger tab and on your address page, where anyone can recompute it.

How long it takes

  • Case opening: immediate — as soon as your signature verifies.
  • Unlock: immediate — in the same pass that opens the case.
  • The wait: about 24 hours (144 Bitcoin blocks — block times vary, so treat this as approximate).
  • Payout: at the first executable window after the wait matures — withdraw, then the transfer to your address.

What the live rehearsal taught us (2026-10-22)

On 2026-10-22 the operator rehearsed this exit live, using the pool's own operating position. The executor halted on a message-wording mismatch right after the unlock had landed; nothing was re-sent blindly and no funds were lost. The rehearsal's real lesson is the wait's cost, which the steps above now state plainly: because unlocks move in whole 0.1 chunks, the pool's claiming deposit dropped from 0.22933277 to 0.12933277 for 27 rounds, and those rounds paid measurably less — a total shortfall of 0.01808455, recorded round by round in a public loss log and allocated per member. The case was cancelled, the full stake was restored on 2026-10-23 (withdrawn, then re-locked whole), and the recorded shortfall still sits in that log awaiting the operator's compensation decision — nothing has been booked or paid without it.

How to verify

  • Hash-chained ledger. Every event — contribution, round settlement, payout, exit — is appended to a hash chain, so editing history would break the chain visibly. The Ledger tab renders it; the raw file (state/live-ledger.json) sits in the repository.
  • Per-round reports. Each settled round publishes a report carrying its own hash (Reports tab), recording the snapshot and every member's share — any round's split can be recomputed from it.
  • Recompute it yourself. The repository's verify tool replays the ledger and checks conservation: total obligations against the pool's on-chain balance and deposit. The pool worker runs the same check on every pass and stops rather than proceed on a mismatch.
  • Fresh, checkable data. This site is regenerated about every 10 minutes from the public RESET gateway; every page carries its snapshot time, and the gateway exposes the same account and round state for cross-checking.
