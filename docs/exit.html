Leaving the pool — the full exit process

Until then the registry accepts no new exit intents and the exit form on the Overview tab is disabled. This page stays up so you can read exactly how an exit works before it reopens.

The principles

  • A right, not an approval. If the ledger shows a position under your address, you may exit it — nobody approves or denies the request. Only an accounting anomaly (the books do not reconcile, or the amount exceeds your recorded position) stops a case for the operator to review.
  • Full exits only. Your principal plus your settled pending earnings, paid in a single payment. There are no partial exits.
  • The payout can only go home. Proceeds are paid only to the address that contributed — the one the ledger records for your position. No field in any request or form can name a different recipient, so a forged request can never redirect your money.
  • The pool pays the transfer fee. The fixed 0.03 TNK fee on the payout transfer is the pool's cost. You receive the full amount; nothing is deducted.
  • The dissolution edge case. If paying one exit would leave the pool's total deposit strictly below 0.1 RESET, that exit escalates to a full dissolution instead: the entire deposit is unlocked and every member is settled in full. Landing exactly on 0.1 does not dissolve the pool.

The process, step by step

  1. Check your position. On the Overview tab (exit section) or the Ledger tab, enter your address to see your principal and pending earnings. That is the position an exit would settle.
  2. File an exit intent. The pool's registry issues a one-time statement with a single-use nonce (valid 30 minutes). Anyone can file the intent for you — including your own AI — because the intent alone proves nothing.
  3. Sign the statement. You sign the exact issued text with your own signer, in the official signed-message format. Signing is free and works at zero balance — your coins sit in the pool, so your wallet may be empty, and no proof transfer is ever asked for. Only your key can produce this signature.
  4. Submit the signature. The signed submission is filed with the registry; the nonce is burned by the first submission, so replays fail. The pool verifies the Ed25519 signature locally, and a case opens. A valid signature from an address with no position opens nothing.
  5. Your amount is frozen. At the moment the case opens, your payout is fixed from the ledger snapshot: principal + pending earnings at that moment. Rounds that settle later are not added to it; any earnings that land afterwards stay as pending under the normal payout rules.
  6. The pool unlocks. The contract only moves stake in single unlocks of at least 0.1, so even a small exit unlocks a full 0.1 (a dissolution unlocks everything). The part above your amount is not paid to anyone — after the wait it comes back to the pool and is re-locked.
  7. Wait 144 Bitcoin blocks — about 24 hours. This wait is the contract's, and it has a real cost, stated plainly: while unlocked, that part of the stake earns nothing, so the whole pool's earnings for those rounds are lower, shared pro-rata by everyone — including you. Your frozen amount itself does not change.
  8. The pool withdraws. Once the wait matures, the pool withdraws the unlocked amount back to its balance. One contract gate applies: a withdrawal is refused while one of the pool's round claims is still unsettled, so the withdrawal fires at the first executable window after maturity — normally right on time, occasionally a little later. The money never expires inside the exit entry.
  9. You are paid in full. The frozen amount is transferred to your original address — the only address it can go to. The 0.03 TNK transfer fee is paid by the pool. If a transfer ever failed on fees, the case would park safely with your RESET intact in the pool balance; the fee is never taken out of your amount.
  10. The position is closed on the public ledger. The close-out is recorded as hash-chained ledger events (verified → unlocked → paid), and the settled exit appears on the Ledger tab and on your address page, where anyone can recompute it.

How long it takes

  • Case opening: immediate — as soon as your signature verifies.
  • Unlock: immediate — in the same pass that opens the case.
  • The wait: about 24 hours (144 Bitcoin blocks — block times vary, so treat this as approximate).
  • Payout: at the first executable window after the wait matures — withdraw, then the transfer to your address.
